Base units (Physics 1): length in metres (m), time in seconds (s), mass in kilograms (kg).

Speed

\boxed{\ \textcolor{#1d4ed8}{3.6\ \tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} = 1\ \tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}}\ } \qquad \text{km/h} \xrightarrow{\ \div 3.6\ } \text{m/s} \qquad \text{m/s} \xrightarrow{\ \times 3.6\ } \text{km/h}

Area and volume: square or cube the factor

1\ \text{m} = 10\ \text{dm} \;\Rightarrow\; 1\ \text{m}^2 = \textcolor{#dc2626}{10^2}\ \text{dm}^2, \qquad 1\ \text{m}^3 = \textcolor{#dc2626}{10^3}\ \text{dm}^3 = 1000\ \text{dm}^3

Useful equals

1\ \text{dm}^3 = 1\ \text{L} \qquad 1\ \text{h} = 3600\ \text{s} \qquad 1\ \text{t} = 1000\ \text{kg} \qquad 1\ \tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3} = 1\ \tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{dm}^3} = 1000\ \tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}

Derived units

\begin{aligned}
F &= m a & [\text{N}] &= \text{kg} \cdot \tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2} \\
W &= F \cdot d & [\text{J}] &= \text{N} \cdot \text{m} \\
P &= \tfrac{W}{t} & [\text{W}] &= \tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{s}} \\
p &= \tfrac{F}{A} & [\text{Pa}] &= \tfrac{\text{N}}{\text{m}^2} \\
\rho &= \tfrac{m}{V} & [\rho] &= \tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}
\end{aligned}

3. Uniform linear motion (v = constant)

\boxed{\ \textcolor{#1d4ed8}{v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{s}{t}}\ } \qquad\qquad t = \frac{s}{v} \qquad\qquad s = v \cdot t

Averages

\text{average speed} = \frac{\text{total } \textcolor{#dc2626}{\text{distance}}}{\text{total time}} \qquad\qquad \text{average velocity} = \frac{\textcolor{#dc2626}{\text{displacement}}}{\text{total time}}

Breaks and stops count in the total time.

Graphs

  • x–t graph: slope = velocity

  • v–t graph: area = displacement (area below the axis is negative)

Example from class (problem 12): +10 m/s for 6 s, then −5 m/s for 4 s.

\text{distance} = 60 + 20 = \textcolor{#15803d}{80 \text{ m}} \qquad \text{displacement} = 60 - 20 = \textcolor{#15803d}{+40 \text{ m}} \qquad \text{avg speed} = \textcolor{#15803d}{8 \tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}} \qquad \text{avg velocity} = \textcolor{#15803d}{+4 \tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}}

4. Average speed

Always works

\boxed{\ \textcolor{#1d4ed8}{\bar{v} = \frac{s_1 + s_2 + \dots}{t_1 + t_2 + \dots}}\ } \qquad t_i = \frac{s_i}{v_i}

Same distance at two speeds (there and back, two halves)

\boxed{\ \textcolor{#1d4ed8}{\bar{v} = \frac{2 v_1 v_2}{v_1 + v_2}}\ } \qquad 40 \text{ and } 60 \tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} \;\rightarrow\; \textcolor{#15803d}{48 \tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}} \ \textcolor{#dc2626}{(\text{not } 50)}

Same time at two speeds (only here can you just average)

\bar{v} = \frac{v_1 + v_2}{2}

Speed needed for the rest of a trip

v = \frac{\text{distance left}}{\text{time left}}

5. Relative motion

\boxed{\ \textcolor{#1d4ed8}{\text{same direction: } v_1 - v_2 \qquad\quad \text{opposite directions: } v_1 + v_2}\ }

Meeting (moving towards each other, d apart)

\textcolor{#1d4ed8}{t = \frac{d}{v_1 + v_2}}

Catching up (gap in front)

\textcolor{#1d4ed8}{t = \frac{\text{gap}}{v_{\text{fast}} - v_{\text{slow}}}}

Always works: write each position and set them equal.

x_A = x_{A0} + v_A t \qquad x_B = x_{B0} + v_B t \qquad x_A = x_B

Example (problem 23): A starts at 0 m going +10 m/s, B starts at 20 m going −6 m/s.

10t = 20 - 6t \;\Rightarrow\; 16t = 20 \;\Rightarrow\; \textcolor{#15803d}{t = 1.25 \text{ s}}, \qquad x = 10 \cdot 1.25 = \textcolor{#15803d}{12.5 \text{ m}}

River (swimmer speed v, current u)

\text{downstream: } v + u \qquad \text{upstream: } v - u
